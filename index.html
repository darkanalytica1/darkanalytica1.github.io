Maritime · Black Sea · 45.12°N 37.02°E

The Black Sea's false ship positions are written on the Russian coast

Shadow-fleet tankers are said to fake where they are. Six months of open vessel tracking for the whole Black Sea, 6.1 million positions from 5,904 vessels, checked against physics, land and each other: hundreds of ships, sanctioned or not, report the same points on land near Kerch and Novorossiysk. Off Romania, the tracks hold.

MethodOpen AIS physics and land checks, sanctions-list joinEvidenceKystverket AIS, six sanctions lists, Black Sea MoUPublished29 Sep 2026

Subsea infrastructure · Arctic · 76.50°N 12.00°E

The Svalbard cable failed on the stretch it cannot hear

Two fibre cables carry almost all of Svalbard's data to the mainland, including SvalSat's satellite downlinks. One failed in January 2022 on the edge of the continental shelf. Open vessel tracking, the published fault record and the physics of repeatered cables show who crosses that route, and what the cable itself could have noticed.

About

I am Leonard Dumitru, based in Bucharest. I research defence and dual-use technology, critical infrastructure, the radio spectrum, and the intelligence and data work around them.

My interest is the space between a capability on paper and a capability that holds up in the field: where a datasheet meets terrain, where a requirement meets a procurement process, where a public record meets what actually happened.

Everything here is built from public sources. Security content is defensive and educational. Where a number matters, its source is next to it; where I am unsure, I say so. Not affiliated with any employer.
